One general knowledge about machine learning is that 
we always hope the models can provide **confident and accurate predictions**. 
Entropy Minimization (EM) is one of the classic methods to achieve this goal. 
It encourages the model to produce **"sharper" outputs** and **high-confidence predictions**, 
which plays a vital role across various machine learning tasks. 
However, the classic EM method is not perfect, 
as its inherent "**<font color="red">coupled</font>**" mechanism limits its potential. 
Through an in-depth analysis of the internal mechanism of entropy minimization, 
we propose a novel **Ada**ptive **D**ecoupled **E**ntropy **M**inimization (**AdaDEM**) method. 
This approach adeptly addresses two major challenges of classical EM, 
the "**<font color="red">reward collapse</font>**" and "**<font color="red">easy-class bias</font>**" phenomena, 
and achieves significant performance gains across multiple ***<font color="green">imperfectly supervised learning</font>*** tasks, 
for example, scarce data labels, shifting data distributions, and presence of noise.

![](./slides/Slides_02.png)

The core idea of Entropy Minimization is to reduce the **uncertainty** in the model's predictions. 
However, we found that the traditional EM loss function is actually a combination of two opposing forces: 
Cluster Aggregation Driving Factor (**CADF**) ***<font color="green">rewards</font>*** classes with higher confidence, 
pushing the model's prediction to **"collapse" toward <font color="red">one extreme</font>**. 
This helps form tight, compact clusters of features. 
Gradient Mitigation Calibrator (**GMC**) acts like a "brake". 
It ***<font color="green">penalizes</font>* classes that are already <font color="red">overconfident</font>**, 
preventing the model from becoming too "self-assured".

![](./slides/Slides_03.png)

This seemingly harmonious coupling, however, hides **<font color="red">two critical flaws</font>**. 
The first one is the **Reward Collapse**. 
When the model's prediction confidence for a sample approaches one, 
the gradient generated by the EM loss rapidly decays to zero. 
This means that ***<font color="green">samples the model has already "learned" contribute almost no further force during subsequent optimization</font>***, 
which limits the potential for additional performance gains. 
The second flaw is the **Easy-Class Bias**. 
Due to the presence of the GMC, EM tends to penalize all high-confidence predictions. 
This causes ***<font color="green">the overall distribution of the model's predicted classes to deviate from the true label distribution</font>***, 
showing a particular bias towards classes that are easier to learn.

![](./slides/Slides_04.png)

To address these two issues, we propose AdaDEM to "decouple" this dilemma. 
To **solve the reward collapse** problem, AdaDEM introduces a very simple yet effective strategy, 
***<font color="green">normalizing the rewards generated by the CADF using the L1-norm</font>***. 
This operation ensures that regardless of how high a sample's initial confidence is, 
the normalized reward signal remains at a stable magnitude. 
This guarantees that <u>even high-confidence samples continue to contribute to the model's optimization</u>, 
thereby breaking through the performance bottleneck.

![](./slides/Slides_05.png)

To **tackle the easy-class bias** problem, 
we designed a Marginal Entropy Calibrator (**MEC**) to replace the original GMC. 
Unlike GMC only focuses on the prediction distribution of a single sample, 
the MEC takes a global perspective. 
***<font color="green">It estimates the marginal class distribution across the entire dataset and uses this estimation to calibrate the gradients.</font>*** 
This allows the model to be aware of **global class information**, 
reducing its excessive preference for certain easy-to-learn classes, 
and <u>ensuring the final output distribution aligns more closely with the true label distribution.</u>

![](./slides/Slides_06.png)

The paper ran a series of detailed tests and proved that AdaDEM really works and can adapt well, 
even **in noisy and constantly changing environments**. 
In both single-domain and continual test-time adaptation (**TTA**) tasks, 
AdaDEM delivered outstanding performance, 
comprehensively ***<font color="green">outperforming a variety of baseline methods, including the classic EM and its upper-bound variant, DEM\*.</font>***
A standout finding is that AdaDEM is ***<font color="green">significantly less sensitive to the learning rate</font>***, 
with its robust learning rate range expanding by **<font color="red">ten</font>** times. 
This means tuning parameters for AdaDEM in practice becomes much easier and more efficient.

![](./slides/Slides_07.png)

Furthermore, the experiments were extended to more challenging scenarios, 
such as semi-supervised learning (**SSL**), 
unsupervised domain adaptation (**UDA**) for semantic segmentation, 
class-imbalanced / long-tail (**LT**) classification, 
and reinforcement learning (**RL**). 
In all these cases, ***<font color="green">AdaDEM demonstrated strong versatility and delivered consistent performance gains</font>***.
